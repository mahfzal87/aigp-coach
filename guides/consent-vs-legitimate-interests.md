# Consent vs legitimate interests: the question is not which is easier

Choose a lawful basis by examining the purpose and conditions, not by reaching for the most convenient checkbox.

## Start with the activity you are justifying

A lawful basis is a reason processing is permitted when its legal conditions are met. It is not a decorative paragraph added to a privacy notice. Before comparing consent and legitimate interests, define the particular purpose, data and people involved.

Neither is the default answer to every scenario. Article 6 contains other bases, such as contract and legal obligation, with their own conditions. Additional rules may apply, including Article 9 for special-category data and rules governing particular communication channels.

## Consent depends on a meaningful choice

Valid consent must be freely given, specific, informed and unambiguous. The circumstances matter: a nominal choice may not be free if refusing it causes an inappropriate detriment or if an imbalance prevents a genuine decision. The person must also be able to withdraw consent as required by the GDPR.

Fictional Coastmere Studio offers an optional, clearly explained research activity that is separate from its core service. Assess whether the actual choice meets the conditions. Do not assume that a checkbox solves unclear wording, unnecessary bundling or an inability to stop the processing.

## Legitimate interests requires reasoning

For legitimate interests, identify a legitimate interest, consider whether the processing is necessary for that interest, and assess whether individuals’ interests or fundamental rights and freedoms override it. Relevant safeguards and reasonable expectations can matter in that analysis.

Fictional Brookfield Market wants to reduce fraudulent orders. That goal is not the end of the exercise. The team must examine the proposed data use, whether a less intrusive approach can achieve the purpose, the effects on people and the protections in place. A useful goal does not justify unlimited collection.

## Avoid switching labels to avoid consequences

Choosing consent creates expectations and obligations. Do not retrospectively switch the same processing to legitimate interests to bypass withdrawn or invalid consent. A genuinely different purpose or processing activity needs its own lawful-basis assessment and the applicable transparency and other requirements.

Likewise, a legitimate-interests assessment should not be a generic template with every answer marked low risk. The evidence must relate to the real activity. If the facts change, an old assessment may no longer explain the current processing.

## How to compare the two in revision

Ask what fact makes one basis available or unsuitable. Is the issue a genuine choice, necessity, expectations, a power imbalance or a conflicting rule? State the condition before naming the basis. That makes your reasoning easier to test.

Finally, separate lawfulness from the other principles. A valid basis does not remove requirements for fairness, minimisation, security or appropriate retention. This article explains a study distinction under the GDPR and is not a recommendation of a basis for any particular business activity.

- Define one purpose precisely.
- Consider all potentially relevant Article 6 bases.
- Test the requirements of the proposed basis.
- Check additional rules and document the reasoning.
