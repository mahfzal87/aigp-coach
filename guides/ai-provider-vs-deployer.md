# You’ve read the AI Act. Can you tell who is responsible?

Provider and deployer are roles attached to facts, not job titles. Work through three fictional situations and see what changes the analysis.

## The distinction that changes the conversation

“We bought it from a vendor” is a useful fact. It is not a complete answer to who is responsible for an AI system. Under the EU AI Act, provider and deployer describe different roles in the AI value chain. One organisation can occupy different roles across different activities.

A provider can develop a system or have it developed, then place it on the market or put it into service under its own name or trademark. A deployer uses a system under its authority, excluding personal non-professional activity. Those are working summaries: apply the full legal definitions and the relevant provisions to real cases.

## Situation one: using a supplied tool

Fictional Northfield Logistics buys a route-planning system and uses it for the purpose described in the supplier’s instructions. The supplier markets the system under its own name. Northfield has not rebranded or changed it.

These facts point towards the supplier being the provider and Northfield being a deployer. That does not imply Northfield has no duties. The obligations depend on the type of system, the applicable provisions and the way Northfield operates it. “The vendor is responsible” is too broad to be a reliable conclusion.

## Situation two: commissioning is still a decision

Fictional Ashbury Analytics pays a development studio to create a system, then releases that system to customers under the Ashbury name. The studio writes every line of code.

The provider definition is not confined to the person who writes the code. Having the system developed and putting it on the market under your name can be decisive. A study answer that follows only the coding work misses the commercial and legal role of the organisation commissioning the system.

## Situation three: a change can change the role

Fictional Merrow Services acquires an AI tool and changes its intended purpose for a new use. Before deciding that it remains only a deployer, the team checks whether the conditions in Article 25 apply. That article addresses circumstances in which certain parties assume provider obligations for high-risk systems, including specified changes of purpose, branding or substantial modification.

Not every configuration adjustment automatically creates provider status. Equally, a contract cannot make a material change irrelevant. The study skill is to identify the missing fact: what changed, which system is involved, and which statutory condition does the change satisfy?

## A reliable way to reason through the roles

Write the activity before the actor: develops; commissions; markets under a name; operates; modifies; changes purpose. Then connect those activities to the relevant definitions. Only after that should you map the duties.

For a real project, verify classification, exceptions and application dates using current legal material. This article focuses on the role distinction and does not determine whether a particular tool is high-risk or which obligations are currently applicable.

- Who supplies the system under their name?
- Who operates it and under whose authority?
- Has its purpose, branding or operation changed in a legally relevant way?
- Which provision, rather than which contract heading, supports the conclusion?
