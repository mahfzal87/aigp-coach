# A privacy checklist is not automatically a DPIA

Understand the relationship between a general privacy review and the GDPR’s data protection impact assessment requirement.

## The name of the document is not decisive

An organisation may review privacy risks for every new product. That is useful governance. But calling a document a “privacy assessment” does not tell you whether it fulfils the GDPR’s requirements for a data protection impact assessment.

A DPIA is required where the relevant processing is likely to result in high risk to people’s rights and freedoms, taking the Article 35 factors into account. Whether a specific activity reaches that threshold requires more than checking whether the project team considers it technically secure.

## A general review can identify the need for a DPIA

Fictional Fairhaven Services has a short intake form for proposed data uses. It asks about purposes, affected people, sensitive information, monitoring, automated decisions and scale. The form helps decide which projects need deeper work.

The intake is a routing tool. It is not automatically the complete DPIA. A low-friction first review can be valuable precisely because it brings privacy into the project early and triggers the right level of assessment rather than pretending every project has already passed.

## A DPIA examines people and processing

Article 35 requires a systematic description of envisaged processing and purposes, an assessment of necessity and proportionality, risks to rights and freedoms, and measures addressing those risks and demonstrating compliance. The legal text includes further requirements and context that should be checked for the activity.

The assessment should be done before the relevant processing. A report written after the product is fully committed is less useful for changing choices. Security controls matter, but the inquiry also concerns whether the processing should happen in the proposed form and what consequences it may have for people.

## Make the assessment influence the decision

Consider fictional Fairhaven proposing extensive monitoring of staff movements. Strong encryption could protect the records from outsiders while leaving the monitoring itself disproportionate. A review restricted to technical confidentiality could miss the central concern.

An assessment might lead to a narrower purpose, less detailed collection, shorter retention, a different design or a decision not to proceed. Record who owns the actions and whether the remaining risk requires escalation. Article 36 addresses prior consultation where its conditions are met; a DPIA is not an automatic permission to launch.

## Revisit the assessment when the facts move

A change in purpose, affected population or technology can make earlier reasoning incomplete. The GDPR requires a review where necessary to assess whether processing is performed in accordance with the DPIA, at least when the risk represented by processing operations changes.

For revision, connect the steps: screen the activity, determine the assessment requirement, examine necessity and risk, choose and implement measures, then review changes. Avoid learning DPIA as simply a longer form. Its value lies in the decisions it informs and the evidence those decisions leave behind.

- General privacy review: an organisational process with a defined scope.
- DPIA: a particular assessment requirement under applicable legal conditions.
- Security assessment: relevant evidence, but potentially narrower in scope.
- Approval: a separate decision that should consider the assessment’s findings.
