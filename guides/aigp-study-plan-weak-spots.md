# Stop rereading the comfortable chapters: a four-week AIGP study plan

An adjustable study routine for finding and repairing weak spots. Four weeks is a planning example, not a promise of exam readiness.

## Make the plan fit the starting point

A useful plan begins with what you can explain and apply today. Four weeks is an example schedule, not a recommended preparation time for everyone. Your experience, available hours and gaps may require a shorter or longer period.

Start with IAPP’s current official Body of Knowledge and exam blueprint. Create a checklist in your own words and identify the areas you have not studied. These public guides cover selected concepts; they are not a substitute for checking the full current scope.

## Week one: build an honest baseline

Choose a few topics from across your study scope. Explain each without looking at notes, then apply it to a fresh fictional example. Mark whether your difficulty was a missing definition, a confused distinction, an incorrect rule or a failure to apply the idea.

Keep a small error log. “I got it wrong” is not specific enough to guide tomorrow’s work. “I treated a management-system standard as a law” is actionable. Record a source that resolves the issue and write the distinction in your own words. Avoid recording recalled real exam content.

## Week two: repair one distinction at a time

Work on concepts that you keep confusing: provider and deployer, validation and monitoring, transparency and explainability. For each pair, write what both have in common and the single feature that separates them in your example.

Then change a fact. If the supplier’s name becomes your organisation’s name, or a recommendation becomes an automated action, does the analysis change? This kind of variation helps reveal whether you understand the decision or have memorised the original wording.

## Week three: connect the lifecycle

Build a fictional launch from the start. Describe the purpose, people affected, data, evaluation evidence, approval conditions and operating controls. Add an incident and a proposed change of purpose. Decide which records, owners and assessments need attention.

Mix topics rather than keeping every session inside a single familiar chapter. Use original practice material with explanations, and review why a plausible alternative is wrong in that specific context. A confident wrong answer deserves at least as much attention as a guess.

## Week four: rehearse and decide what still needs work

Use timed practice where suitable, but separate the score from the quality of your reasoning. Look for recurring weak areas, rushed reading and uncertainty about scope. Revisit the sources for errors rather than merely repeating the same questions until their answers become familiar.

Before deciding to sit the exam, compare your coverage with the official blueprint and consider your performance across unfamiliar tasks. A short quiz, streak or completion badge cannot establish readiness on its own. If the evidence still shows major gaps, extend the plan.

- A short session: recall one concept, solve one new scenario, review one error.
- A weekly review: inspect patterns, choose the next priority, update your schedule.
- A useful milestone: explain why the close alternative fails, without relying on the answer key.
