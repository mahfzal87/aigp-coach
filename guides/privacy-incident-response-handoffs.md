# Incident, breach, escalation: who does what first?

A fictional tabletop exercise for privacy managers: coordinate containment, assessment and decisions without confusing technical response with legal notification.

## Separate the event from the conclusion

An incident report is a signal to investigate. It is not automatically proof of a reportable personal data breach. A personal data breach can concern confidentiality, integrity or availability; it is not restricted to stolen files.

The organisation needs facts quickly enough to make timely decisions under applicable requirements. Avoid two extremes: notifying every audience before understanding anything, or waiting for complete forensic certainty before starting the assessment. The relevant legal thresholds and timing still need to be evaluated.

## The fictional exercise

At fictional Fenwick Services, an employee reports that a shared link may expose a customer spreadsheet outside the intended team. Nobody yet knows whether it was accessed. The service owner, security team and privacy lead are available.

The immediate work includes reducing ongoing exposure, preserving appropriate evidence and establishing the scope. Who has authority to restrict the link? Who can determine which records were involved? Who records when the organisation became aware of relevant facts? These tasks can run in coordination rather than waiting for a long sequential approval chain.

## Containment and privacy analysis need each other

Security investigates the exposure and helps contain it. The relevant business team understands the records and process. Privacy and legal expertise helps assess consequences for people and applicable obligations. Communications must use facts that have been checked.

A privacy manager should not assume that closing the technical issue closes the incident. Nor should a technical team be expected to decide every notification question alone. Define the handoffs in advance so that expertise and authority are available when needed.

## Record decisions, including decisions not to notify

Under the GDPR, Articles 33 and 34 distinguish supervisory-authority notification and communication to affected individuals, with different conditions. Processor-to-controller notification is another relevant relationship. Do not collapse these into one generic “notify everyone” step.

For the fictional exercise, document known facts, uncertainty, risk reasoning, decisions, owners and updates. If the assessment changes as evidence emerges, the record should show why the response changed. This guide does not calculate a deadline or decide reportability for a real incident.

## Recovery is not the end of the lesson

After the exposure is controlled, examine why it happened. Was the sharing default inappropriate? Was the approved workflow too difficult? Did people know how to report? A corrective action should address the relevant cause and have a later effectiveness check.

Run the same exercise with a different fact: the spreadsheet is not disclosed but becomes unavailable during a critical service. The analysis will differ, yet availability remains relevant. That variation helps prevent a narrow mental model in which privacy incidents always mean a public leak.

- Detect and route the signal.
- Contain harm while preserving useful evidence.
- Assess people, data, scope and applicable duties.
- Make and document notification decisions.
- Recover, address causes and check the corrective actions.
