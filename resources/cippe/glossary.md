# CIPP/E glossary

## Personal data

Information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person. Identification can be direct or indirect, depending on the information and means reasonably likely to be used.

**Example:** An employee number linked to a staff directory relates to an identifiable person.

**Keep separate:** Removing a name does not necessarily anonymise a record.

## Processing

An operation performed on personal data, such as collecting, using, disclosing, storing or deleting it. The GDPR concept is broader than analysing data.

**Example:** Keeping an archived spreadsheet is still a form of processing.

**Keep separate:** Storage is not outside privacy rules merely because nobody currently reads the file.

## Controller

The party that determines why and how personal data is processed, assessed against the actual activity. Legal role analysis considers purposes and essential means.

**Example:** A retailer decides to collect customer details to manage its loyalty programme.

**Keep separate:** A supplier does not become a controller solely because it operates the servers.

## Processor

A party processing personal data on behalf of a controller. Its relationship and activities must meet the applicable GDPR requirements.

**Example:** A hosting provider stores a customer database under its client’s instructions.

**Keep separate:** A supplier’s independent reuse of data needs separate analysis.

## Joint controllers

Parties that jointly determine purposes and means of processing. Their arrangement must allocate relevant responsibilities without removing individuals’ rights.

**Example:** Two organisations jointly design a common data-collection activity and decide its purpose.

**Keep separate:** Exchanging data does not automatically make every participant a joint controller.

## Lawful basis

A ground under Article 6 that permits processing when its requirements are met. The basis must fit the specific purpose and facts.

**Example:** An organisation identifies a legal obligation for a particular statutory record.

**Keep separate:** A privacy notice explains processing; it does not create a lawful basis.

## Consent

A valid expression of choice meeting GDPR requirements, including being freely given, specific, informed and unambiguous. Some processing requires explicit consent.

**Example:** A person makes a separate optional choice about a specific use of their data.

**Keep separate:** Silence or a bundled non-negotiable term is not automatically valid consent.

## Legitimate interests

An Article 6 basis that requires identifying an interest, assessing necessity and balancing it against individuals’ interests and rights. Additional restrictions may apply.

**Example:** An organisation assesses a proportionate fraud-prevention activity and records safeguards.

**Keep separate:** An activity being useful to a business does not complete the balancing exercise.

## Special-category data

Categories identified in Article 9, such as health data and certain biometric data. Processing generally needs an applicable Article 9 condition as well as an Article 6 basis.

**Example:** A service processes medical information about customers.

**Keep separate:** Not every photograph is special-category biometric data; purpose and processing matter.

## Pseudonymisation

Processing that separates data from additional information needed to attribute it to a person, with appropriate measures protecting that additional information.

**Example:** Research records use codes while a separately protected file holds the identities.

**Keep separate:** Pseudonymised data can remain personal data.

## Anonymisation

Making information no longer relate to an identifiable person, assessed in context against means reasonably likely to be used. It is a substantive outcome, not a label.

**Example:** A carefully assessed aggregate may avoid revealing identifiable individuals.

**Keep separate:** A hashed identifier or removal of direct identifiers does not automatically achieve anonymity.

## Data minimisation

Limiting personal data to what is adequate, relevant and necessary for the processing purpose. It requires understanding the purpose first.

**Example:** A booking process omits a field that is unrelated to providing the booking.

**Keep separate:** Keeping fewer fields does not fix an unlawful purpose.

## Storage limitation

Keeping identifiable data only for as long as necessary for the relevant purpose, subject to applicable exceptions and safeguards.

**Example:** A retention schedule is implemented in active systems and considered in backups.

**Keep separate:** A retention policy with no deletion process is incomplete implementation.

## DPIA

A data protection impact assessment used to assess and address risks in processing likely to create high risk to people’s rights and freedoms.

**Example:** A team assesses a proposed monitoring activity before it begins.

**Keep separate:** A DPIA is distinct from a generic cybersecurity checklist.

## Data protection by design

Building appropriate data-protection measures into the design and operation of processing, with the factors in Article 25 taken into account.

**Example:** A new service is designed to minimise data exposure and enforce access boundaries.

**Keep separate:** A notice added after launch does not by itself achieve protection by design.

## Personal data breach

A security breach leading to accidental or unlawful loss, destruction, alteration, unauthorised disclosure of, or access to, personal data.

**Example:** A corrupted database makes necessary personal records unavailable.

**Keep separate:** A breach is not limited to information being stolen or leaked.

## Data portability

A right to receive and, in applicable circumstances, transmit certain personal data in a usable format when the Article 20 conditions apply.

**Example:** An eligible user requests provided data from an automated service based on contract.

**Keep separate:** Portability is not identical to the broader right of access.

## International transfer safeguard

A mechanism or protection used in the Chapter V analysis for relevant transfers of personal data. The appropriate route depends on destination and circumstances.

**Example:** A business assesses a transfer and the suitability of contractual safeguards.

**Keep separate:** An Article 6 lawful basis is not, by itself, a Chapter V transfer mechanism.
