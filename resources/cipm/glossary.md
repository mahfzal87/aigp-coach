# CIPM glossary

## Privacy programme

An organisation’s coordinated approach to managing privacy responsibilities, risks and practices over time. It includes people, decisions, processes and evidence.

**Example:** A business connects procurement reviews, rights handling and incident escalation under common oversight.

**Keep separate:** A policy document is part of a programme, not the programme itself.

## Programme charter

A document establishing a programme’s purpose, scope, authority and operating expectations. Its value depends on whether those arrangements work in practice.

**Example:** Leadership approves decision rights and resources for a privacy function.

**Keep separate:** A charter does not replace detailed procedures or operational ownership.

## Executive sponsor

A senior person who supports the programme’s mandate and helps resolve resource or organisational barriers.

**Example:** A sponsor ensures privacy issues receive attention in a major transformation.

**Keep separate:** Sponsorship does not make every operational task the sponsor’s personal responsibility.

## Accountability

Being responsible for privacy obligations and able to demonstrate appropriate action. Under the GDPR, accountability has a specific role in the principles.

**Example:** A team can show its decisions, controls and checks rather than only assert compliance.

**Keep separate:** Assigning an owner does not transfer away every other party’s duties.

## Data inventory

A maintained record of relevant data or processing activities and their context, such as purpose, systems, recipients and retention.

**Example:** An inventory links a customer process to its systems and service providers.

**Keep separate:** An asset list alone may omit why and how personal data is used.

## Data flow map

A representation of where information is collected, used, transferred and stored across a process. It helps reveal dependencies and overlooked disclosures.

**Example:** A map shows a support export leaving the central application for a vendor.

**Keep separate:** A network diagram may not describe the business purpose or people affected.

## Risk owner

The person or function accountable for managing a specified risk and making or escalating relevant decisions within their authority.

**Example:** A service owner is responsible for addressing privacy risks in a customer workflow.

**Keep separate:** The privacy adviser is not automatically the owner of every business risk.

## Control

A measure intended to change risk or support an obligation. It may be technical, organisational or procedural.

**Example:** A system restricts access, while a process periodically checks whether access remains justified.

**Keep separate:** Writing a requirement is not evidence that the control operates.

## Residual risk

The risk remaining after controls are taken into account. It should be evaluated and accepted or escalated under defined authority.

**Example:** A temporary limitation remains after a remediation and has an explicit review date.

**Keep separate:** An overdue remediation is not an implicit approval to accept the risk.

## Privacy assessment

A structured review of an activity’s privacy implications. Its scope depends on the organisation’s method and applicable obligations.

**Example:** A product review considers purpose, data use, transparency and risk to individuals.

**Keep separate:** Not every internal assessment fulfils the legal requirements of a DPIA.

## Vendor due diligence

Checks used to understand a supplier’s relevant practices, capabilities and risks before and during a relationship.

**Example:** A buyer examines how a supplier handles requests, security incidents and deletion.

**Keep separate:** A completed questionnaire is evidence to evaluate, not proof that all controls work.

## Retention schedule

Rules explaining how long relevant categories of records are kept and what happens at the end of that period.

**Example:** A schedule ties a record category to a purpose, trigger and disposal action.

**Keep separate:** The schedule must be implemented; publishing it alone does not remove data.

## Rights-request workflow

The process for recognising, assessing, responding to and documenting an individual’s privacy request under applicable rules.

**Example:** Support staff route a request to a team that can identify data and coordinate a response.

**Keep separate:** A helpdesk ticket is not automatically a legally complete response.

## Incident response

Coordinated work to investigate, contain and address an event, including relevant notification decisions and recovery.

**Example:** Security and privacy teams assess an exposed record set and document their response.

**Keep separate:** Not every incident is a reportable personal data breach.

## Key performance indicator

A measure used to understand performance against an objective. A useful KPI connects a result to a decision or action.

**Example:** The programme tracks whether high-risk reviews are completed before launch.

**Keep separate:** A count of completed reviews lacks context without the number that required review.

## Key risk indicator

A signal intended to help identify changes in risk exposure or conditions requiring attention.

**Example:** A rising backlog of high-priority remediation is escalated to the responsible owner.

**Keep separate:** A risk signal is not a precise prediction that an incident will occur.

## Control testing

Checking whether a control is designed appropriately and operates as intended, using evidence suited to the control.

**Example:** A sample of closed accounts is checked for actual access removal.

**Keep separate:** An interview about a process is not the same as observing its operation.

## Corrective action

An action addressing an identified problem or cause, with follow-up to establish whether it worked.

**Example:** A recurring routing failure leads to a revised intake process and a later effectiveness check.

**Keep separate:** Closing a ticket does not establish that the underlying cause was removed.
